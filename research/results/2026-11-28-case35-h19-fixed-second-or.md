## Count operand traces below 129 rows

Fix any one selected operand at each of the eight ordinary ANDs. These eight observations distinguish the negative rows in a certified clique, for every orientation of the eight operand pairs (the existing incompatible-negative trace lemma). Let W be the total packing width over its k rows. A row with f packed flags forces at least f−1 false OR outputs. Thus a lower bound T on the total number of true OR entries gives an OR floor of ceil((W+T)/k)−1.

*Repeated physical source.* Suppose two different receivers use the same physical input. Select that source at both. The other six selected bits together with the common bit must encode k distinct seven-bit words. Another duplicate gives at most 64 possibilities. If one of the other receivers selects an earlier ordinary AND's output, that bit implies the bit selected at the parent. The parent and child select different physical sources by acyclicity. Thus either a second equality already holds, or two of the seven bits satisfy an implication, allowing at most 96 words. Every k exceeds 96. Each raw starting column is constant on a certified clique of at least 65 rows; selecting it as one of the other six bits allows at most 64 codes on that clique. There is also a guard-false clique of at least 65 rows, which rules out choosing a mask in one of those six slots. Hence both inputs of each of the other six receivers are OR outputs. No two of these six receivers can share an input, since its two ends could be selected along with the original repeated pair. Use complementary orientations of those six gates; their charged ORs are distinct across both traces. A redundant gate with equal inputs can be deleted. With fewer than eight nonredundant ordinary ANDs, pad with copies of a nonredundant AND (or with unused ANDs of distinct starting inputs), giving a repeated source at different receivers and distinct inputs within each receiver. Sum twice the k lightest Hamming weights on six charged bits of a seven-bit word. The resulting floor is 22 ORs for every pair.

*No repeated physical source.* Regard each selected earlier-AND output as an edge from its producing ordinary AND to its receiver. Each vertex selects at most one input. Each produced wire can be selected at most once. Acyclicity therefore partitions the eight vertices into directed paths; an edge requires the child's bit to imply its parent's bit. Each root charges an OR unless it is an exceptional port (an initial wire or guard output). Two different receivers cannot both select a mask: on the guard-false clique that would leave at most 64 traces. Two abstract mask ports therefore suffice. For a subset of s exceptional ports at distinct receivers, the verifier rejects any simultaneous constant fiber of more than 2^(8−s) incompatible rows. Enumerating partitions into singletons and pairs bounds the number of exceptional ports by four for 24 graphs and five for two. Orient complementary traces to split them as evenly as possible, including putting opposite inputs of one receiver on opposite sides. Enumerate the integer partitions of eight into path lengths, all eight-bit words respecting the edges, and every possible set of unpaid roots. Take the sum of the k smallest charged weights for each orientation. A five-port limit permits at most two unpaid roots on one side and three on the other. The 24 four-port cases need at least 20 ORs, and the two five-port cases need at least 19. All floors exceed the eighteen ORs available.
